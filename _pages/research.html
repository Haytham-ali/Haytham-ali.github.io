My research focuses on computational imaging and inverse problems, with particular emphasis on reconstructing high-quality CT images from incomplete projection data. I combine mathematical modeling, imaging physics, numerical optimization, and deep learning for challenging acquisition settings such as sparse-view and limited-angle CT.

A central theme of my work is the integration of learned image priors with explicit knowledge of the measurement process. I am particularly interested in projection-domain constraints, data-consistency mechanisms, attention, and frequency-aware representations for physically meaningful reconstruction.

Research Directions

01

Sparse-View CT Reconstruction

Sparse-view CT reduces the number of acquired projections, making the reconstruction problem severely ill-posed. Insufficient angular sampling can produce streak artifacts and loss of fine structural information.

My work investigates generative and physics-informed reconstruction frameworks that combine learned image priors with explicit consistency with measured projection data. Recent directions include dual-stage architectures, attention mechanisms, adaptive feature gating, and differentiable data-consistency steps.

Representative work
  • PIDA-GAN: Physics-Informed Dual-Stage Attention GAN with Data Consistency for Sparse-View CT Reconstruction — Physics in Medicine & Biology, 2026.
  • PCDS-GAN: Physics-Constrained Dual-Stage Generative Model with Adaptive Gating for Ultra Sparse-View CT Reconstruction — IEEE ISBI 2026, Oral.
  • Novel Dual-Stage GAN for High-Fidelity CT Image Reconstruction from Sparse-View CT — JAMIT 2025.
02

Limited-Angle CT Reconstruction

Limited-angle CT uses projection measurements acquired over a restricted angular range. The missing angular information creates direction-dependent artifacts and structural ambiguities that cannot be fully addressed by conventional filtered back-projection.

I investigate frequency-aware learning, locality-guided attention, and generative reconstruction for this problem, with emphasis on representing both local image structure and long-range contextual information.

Representative work

  • Multi-Frequency and Locality-Guided Attention U-Net GAN for Limited-Angle CT Reconstruction — IEEE ISBI 2026; Best Poster Award: Runner Up.
  • Attention-Guided Frequency and Locality Learning GAN for Limited-Angle CT Reconstruction — IWAIT 2026 / SPIE.
  • An Attention-Based Generative Adversarial Network for Limited-Angle CT Reconstruction — ICCAE 2025; Best Presentation Award.
03

Physics-Guided Deep Learning

A recurring methodological theme in my research is the integration of imaging physics with deep neural networks. Rather than treating reconstruction purely as image-to-image translation, I study architectures that incorporate forward and back-projection operators, projection-domain constraints, and explicit data consistency.

This direction also includes attention-based feature modeling and frequency-domain representations for incomplete-data reconstruction.

Representative work
  • PIDA-GAN — Physics in Medicine & Biology, 2026.
  • PCDS-GAN — IEEE ISBI 2026.
  • FLoAT-GAN — AAAI-26 W3PHIAI Workshop, 2026.
04

Electron Tomography

Electron tomography presents reconstruction challenges closely related to sparse-view and limited-angle CT, including restricted angular coverage and a limited number of projections.

My JSPS fellowship research focused on ultra-sparse-view electron tomography and reconstruction strategies that combine model-based methods with learning-based approaches. This provides a natural bridge between reconstruction principles developed for X-ray CT and their application to three-dimensional electron-microscopy data.

05

Model-Based Reconstruction & Level-Set Methods

My earlier research focused on mathematical and optimization-based approaches to limited-data tomography. In particular, I studied parametric level-set representations for binary and discrete tomography, where object boundaries are modeled implicitly and prior knowledge about image structure is incorporated directly into the reconstruction problem.

This work forms an important mathematical foundation for my current interest in hybrid approaches combining explicit models with learned representations.

Current Research Interests

Where the work is heading

My current interests include physics-guided generative reconstruction, sinogram-domain learning, frequency-aware and attention-based architectures, and reconstruction from extremely limited measurements. I am also interested in extending these ideas toward multimodal and foundation-model approaches for medical imaging while maintaining a clear connection to the underlying imaging physics.
